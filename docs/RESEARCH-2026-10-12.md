# Research: how good can BERT be? — 2026-10-12

Baseline: v0.8.1 (code 20), app source `ef28193`. Five independent reviews: product/UX, engineering, Android + Play standards,
character-companion benchmark, crypto-tracker benchmark. A sixth reviewer then fact-checked all 64 findings against the repo
and the cited sources: 50 confirmed, 12 partly right, 1 wrong, 1 unverifiable. The full findings, with file:line and URLs,
are in [evidence/2026-10-12/research-findings.json](evidence/2026-10-12/research-findings.json). Read-only research. Nothing
was built, changed or deployed.

## Verdict

The engineering, privacy stance and data honesty are above the bar for a Play production release. The product is not yet a
premium character app. It is a tidy dashboard that happens to have a mascot. A new user's first screen has the same Bert photo
twice, a greeting that never changes and two lines of plumbing. Nothing changes from day 1 to day 100. The best asset (The
Lost Trail) sits behind the second tab. The price, which holders check first, is two taps deep, and every widget or
notification tap lands on a Home screen with no price. Most fixes are small or medium and need no accounts and no new
infrastructure.

**Keep:** the honest freshness and stale states, the no-account, on-device privacy posture, curated outbound links, the
large-text handling, the widget render tests, the caption-card flow, and the Lost Trail art and offline play.

## Time-sensitive (decide this week)

1. **Play app-signing key.** If Play holds a Google-generated signing key, sideloaded users must uninstall, losing their
   holdings and cards, to move to Play. The signing key can be changed to the existing release key only before the app
   is released to open testing or production (Play Help answer/9842756). Then Play builds would install over berthalla.io
   APKs and keep the data. *Owner to check Play Console → App integrity.*
2. **The 12-tester / 14-day clock has not started.** Internal testing does not count. The gate requires a **closed**
   testing track with 12 or more testers continuously opted in for 14 days (answer/14151465). Create the closed track,
   move the tester form's link to it, recruit about 20, ship one tester-driven update during the 14 days and keep a
   feedback log. The production-access form asks about all of this.
3. **Privacy and contact gaps that Play requires.** There is no in-app privacy-policy link (the User Data policy requires
   one inside the app), the listing has no contact email, and the privacy page has no server-log or retention text. Also,
   Bert's-feed requests send the phone model in the user agent (BERTActivityRepository bypasses BERTHttp), and nginx logs
   it with the IP.

## Decision needed before building "living Bert"

**Bert's mood is computed from price.** `/opt/berthalla/berthalla/mood/state.py` derives the mood from BERT and BTC 24h
change. Several ideas (mood photos on the widget, mood greetings, "Bert is feeling smug" notifications) would turn the
real dog's face into a market indicator, which contradicts the "informational only" stance. Choose one: decouple mood
from price on the server (time of day, events, dispatch tone), or keep mood off imagery and notifications.

## Corrections the fact-check made

- DEX Screener documents 300 requests/min on token-pairs, so the "undocumented rate limit" finding was wrong.
- Proposed candle-wick alert checks don't work as described: stored history keeps only closes.
- material3 1.5.0 is beta (7 Oct 2026), not alpha. Stable is 1.4.0.
- The Bert widget's picker preview is a reasonable static mock. Only the two price widgets show a placeholder.
- The Home tournament card already has an ended state. What's missing is a results story and start/finish notices.
- Developer verification (enforced in BR/ID/SG/TH since 30 Sep 2026) applies only to certified devices in those
  countries for now. Registering the sideloaded package is cheap insurance before the 2027 global rollout.
